# P6 · Private ambulance pricing and dispatch
Working name: "Saarthi" (rename if it collides)

## Context (verify everything; Claude Code must research this one from scratch)
- Public 108/102 services exist but families often call private ambulances, especially for inter-hospital transfers and in cities.
- Reported problems: unclear pricing, surge charges in emergencies, unknown equipment level (BLS vs ALS), unverified crews, no tracking, payment disputes.
- Find: any state rules or price caps (e.g. Karnataka, Delhi, Maharashtra), National Ambulance Code (AIS-125) equipment standards, Clinical Establishments Act provisions, news reports on overcharging. Cite each.

## Problem
In an emergency, families have no time to compare and no power to negotiate. They don't know if the ambulance that arrives has the right equipment, what it will cost, or when it will come.

## Users
Anitha (45, father had a stroke at home, Hubballi), Rahul (31, needs an ICU transfer between hospitals in Bengaluru), Joseph (operator with 6 ambulances), hospital emergency coordinator; stakeholder: state health department.

## Scope ideas
One-tap request with condition triage (in plain words, not medical jargon), upfront fixed price or capped range, equipment level and crew certification shown, live tracking with ETA confidence, nearest-capable-hospital guidance, family sharing, post-trip invoice vs quote check, operator dispatch console, rating and complaint flow.

## Trade-offs
Speed vs information in an emergency; fixed price vs operator viability; triage advice vs medical liability (we never diagnose); offline and low-signal use.
